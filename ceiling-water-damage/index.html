Ceiling Water Damage Repair in Ocala

When a brown circle finally appears above you, the leak has been at work for a while. Long before you see discoloration, the insulation is wet, moisture has followed the framing and a puddle sits on the hidden side of the panel. The leak may be a bathroom on the floor above, a pipe in the ceiling, an air conditioner drain line or the roof. We trace it to the source before anything gets patched.

Covering the mark with paint is not a repair. Moisture left in the cavity brings the discoloration back and lets mold get going on the paper side in 24 to 48 hours. The opening is kept as small as the damage allows, wet insulation comes out, joists and surrounding drywall are dried, and the area is patched, textured and painted to blend. Sagging changes the picture. Stay out from under it and call.

Warning Signs

  • Patches of spotty discoloration that look like mold on the ceiling
  • Hairline cracks spreading outward from a stained or damp spot
  • Discolored rings overhead that get bigger each time it rains or the bathroom is used
  • Bits of textured finish falling to the floor below a damp patch
  • Water dripping from a light fixture, vent or smoke detector
  • Blistered, bubbling or flaking paint on the ceiling
What We Do

What Happens After You Call

1

Find the Source

Using thermal imaging and moisture meters, our team follows the water to its origin and inspects the attic or room overhead. The leak has to be stopped before repairs make sense.

2

Secure the Room

Power to wet fixtures is shut off and furniture below is covered or moved. If the ceiling is holding water, we release it in a controlled way and catch it.

3

Open and Dry

Damaged drywall and wet insulation come out. The joists and nearby ceiling are dried with air movers plus a dehumidifier, and antimicrobial goes on wherever the water was dirty.

4

Rebuild and Paint

When everything reads dry, our crew replaces the insulation, hangs drywall, finishes the seams, blends the texture with the rest of the ceiling, then applies stain blocker and paint.

The Clock

How the Damage Progresses

The first few hours

Water Spreads Above

The insulation gets wet as water travels down the framing. Because the panel soaks from its top side, nothing may look wrong from underneath.

24 to 48 hours

Stains and Bubbles

You see the first ring, along with blisters in the paint. Mold can begin on the drywall paper and the wet insulation presses down on the panel.

Day 3 to 7

The Ceiling Bows

Softened gypsum loses its hold on the screws. A stale odor drifts into the room as seams separate and the drywall sags in the spans between framing.

After a week

Risk of Collapse

Parts of the ceiling can come down along with the wet insulation resting on them. Mold is established in the cavity and the repair now covers a much larger area.

Behind the Surface

The Damage You Do Not See

Asbestos in Older Ceilings

Asbestos was used in certain older plasters and popcorn finishes. Testing comes first in such a home, ahead of any cutting, scraping or demolition of the damaged section.

Wet Lights and Wiring

Recessed lights, fans and junction boxes sit right in the wet zone. Water in a fixture can short the circuit, so switch off the breaker for that room.

Sudden Collapse

Wet drywall is far heavier than dry and loses its grip on the fasteners. An entire piece can fall from a bulging spot onto anything below.

Damage on the Floor Above

Whatever is over the ceiling, a subfloor or attic decking, has typically been soaked as well. Leaving it out of the drying plan lets dampness linger, so both levels get inspected.
